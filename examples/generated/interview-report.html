Key moments

Initial structure

I want to look at footfall, conversion and basket size, and separately at shrink in fresh.
What you did

Presented a four-branch structure after roughly 90 seconds of think time and named fresh as the starting point.

What worked

The branches are the genuine drivers of this format, and a priority was given with a reason attached.

The problem

No expectation was stated — the structure said where to look but not what the candidate thought would be found.

Downstream effect

The analysis became a sequential check of each branch rather than a test of a hypothesis.

Stronger handling

Adding 'I expect the problem is in fresh, because it carries the highest margin and the highest spoilage risk' would have given every subsequent step something to confirm or kill.

Evidence: View T04

Exhibit 2 — margin by category

What you did

Read the margin rate for each of the four categories in turn.

The problem

The gaps were never quantified and the 'so what' was never asked.

Downstream effect

Missed that frozen carries an above-average margin while losing share — the contradiction the case is built on.

Stronger handling

Compute that frozen sits about six points above the blended margin, then note its share is falling anyway; that points straight at placement or replenishment rather than demand.

Evidence: View T09 View T10 View T12

Insights you didn't reach

The highest-margin category shrinking means this is not a demand problem

Evidence available

Exhibit 2 category margin rates, and Exhibit 1 category share-of-sales over three years.

Where you stopped

Both exhibits were read correctly, but as two separate facts.

What follows from it

Frozen carries the highest margin and is losing share while lower-margin dry goods gains it. That is a mix shift, not a footfall problem.

Why it matters to the client

The client arrived planning to spend on promotions to drive traffic. The mix reading points instead at placement and replenishment, which costs roughly an order of magnitude less and does not further dilute margin.

Your recommendation vs a stronger one

What you said

Where it falls short

Answers the question asked
The client asked why profit fell. This opens with what to do and skips the diagnosis.
Evidence density
Three supporting reasons, one number.
Risks
No mention that promotions would compress margin further — the opposite of the stated objective.
Next steps
'Reduce shrink' is a goal, not an executable next step.

A stronger version

Profit fell because of category mix, not footfall: frozen dropped from 22% to 15% of sales while dry goods, six margin points lower, took its place, and that shift alone accounts for roughly two thirds of the margin gap. I would therefore not increase promotional spend, which would compress margin further. I would start with a placement and replenishment review of the frozen category. The main risk is that the share loss is demand-driven rather than execution-driven, in which case merchandising changes will not recover it. Next step: pull twelve months of frozen out-of-stock rates and shelf-space allocation, which is enough to separate the two within two weeks.

What to train next

Move exhibits from description to implication

Where you are

Charts are read accurately, then left as observations.

Why it matters

The exhibit is usually where a case turns. Stopping at description discards the strongest available evidence.

Drill

Run five exhibit reps. For each, state one observation and one insight, and the insight must cite a number you computed yourself from the chart.

Raise the evidence density of the recommendation

Where you are

Answer-first delivery is solid; the supporting reasons are not quantified.

Why it matters

An unquantified reason reads as an assertion, and assertions do not survive a partner's follow-up.

Drill

Run three synthesis reps: given the case facts, deliver only the 90-second recommendation, under the rule that every supporting reason must carry a number that appeared in the case.
